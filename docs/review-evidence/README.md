# Review implementation evidence — September 7, 2026

These are measured development results from an Apple M5 Max, macOS ARM64,
Rust 1.90.0 and Node 26.8.1. They describe the review working tree based on
`fa3d11976938dbeeb9f8def3d69a3e6eb526b876`, not a clean release or physical-mobile
certification. [Source hashes](source-manifest.json) identify the final tested
source; [built asset hashes](tested-build.json) identify the browser build.
Earlier throughput captures precede final observation/UI-only fixes; the tested
physics and force algorithms are unchanged. Raw trajectories are regenerated by
the qualification commands and retained as CI artifacts, rather than committed.

## Six delivered priorities

1. **Long-run numerical qualification.** Required release checks now include
   600-year worlds, a 4,096-year eccentric orbit, timestep refinement, an
   independent SciPy DOP853 reference, orbital phase/apsis diagnostics, and a
   persistent 512-body tree/direct comparison. The checks exposed and fixed
   inadequate moon resolution and inward disk migration beyond the resolved
   inner edge. [Numerical results](numerical.json), [tree lifetime](tree-lifetime.json).
2. **Fast, validated restart.** Bounded sandbox checkpoints carry exact-build
   provenance and payload hashes. Imports validate state and replay membership;
   missing/corrupt caches fall back to command reconstruction. Named saves wait
   for persistence. Campaigns continue to reconstruct their command records.
3. **Useful long histories.** Bounded priority and recent histories, eight
   user-pinned bodies, independently sampled population summaries, and sampled
   flyby records with before/after orbital readings are exposed in the UI.
4. **Controlled comparisons.** Same-age body overlays, shared-scale charts,
   retained-impact ancestry, and up to three one-condition variants preserve
   the original experiment. A newer comparison supersedes an older request.
5. **Device and recovery qualification.** Exact-build physical-device reports
   require five active minutes at the specified quality, frame-time and pace
   thresholds. Reload, corrupted-cache fallback and worker recovery are tested.
   The [physical-device matrix](physical-devices.json) remains pending: browser
   emulation does not count as an iPhone or iPad hardware test.
6. **Large-count algorithm experiments.** f64 SIMD parity, error-estimator tree
   acceptance, resident WebGPU, mixed precision and a changed semi-active
   particle model have reproducible measurements. See the
   [primary-source literature review](../ALGORITHM_RESEARCH.md).

## Numerical results and limits

All production qualification fixtures pass their stated budgets. Maximum sampled
relative energy-balance error over 600 years is 9.67e-13 for the moon family,
6.83e-7 for the resonant giants, 6.38e-6 for migration/escape, and 6.54e-6 for
the initially 1,024-body massive swarm. The swarm ends with 453 bodies after
mergers; it is observed above the tree threshold for 475 years.

The persistent 512-body collisionless tree fixture lasts all 600 years. Its final
tree/direct position RMS difference is 0.000280 AU and maximum relative energy
error is 8.18e-12. Its debris mass is deliberately low, 0.016 Earth masses:
this isolates tree accumulation and does not certify arbitrary dense encounters.

Against the independent reference, sampled moon phase error drops from about
0.248 to 0.0154 radians. Migration/escape energy-balance error drops from about
0.154 to 6.38e-6. These fixes change the physics identifier: authored moons use
16 fixed substeps and migration uses 32, with torque smoothly tapering to zero
inside 0.25 AU. Ordinary swarms retain four substeps. The 4,096-year eccentric
fixture passes its conservation and refinement checks; independent orbital
accuracy is qualified for 600 years, not the full 4,096 years.

## Performance decisions

| Experiment | Measured result | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Existing f64 SIMD | 286 bitwise-identical scalar/SIMD checkpoints; 1.38× scalar throughput at 64 bodies in this capture | Retain; this is confirmation of existing SIMD, not a new speedup |
| Complete swarm ticks, baseline versus review | Identical body states and balances in all ten cases; 512–8,192-body throughput within about 2% of baseline; 64-body observation overhead about 6–11% | Large-count throughput retained while adding observations |
| Error-estimator tree, 8,192 clustered bodies | Tight tolerance reduces p99 nonstellar force error from 0.0273 to 0.000367; force cost rises from 2.37 to 9.48 ms | Experimental; no production rule change |
| Resident GPU, 8,192 bodies | 2.925 versus 10.2875 ms/tick, about 3.52× faster in the final short collisionless capture | Experimental: incomplete game pipeline and fails lifetime accuracy |
| Resident GPU moon lifetime | Rejected at year 15: orbital-size error 0.002989 exceeds 0.002 despite energy error remaining below 2e-5 | Disabled in live physics |
| f64-state / f32-distant-force GPU | At 1,024 bodies, 101.1 versus 10.5 ms per eight ticks, including transfers | Accurate short probe, slower; disabled in live physics |
| Native semi-active model, 8,192 bodies / eight active | 0.634 versus 14.95 ms/tick; 1,024-body changed-model energy error stays below 3.59e-7 over 600 years | Research only: omits passive/passive gravity; not equivalent accretion physics |

Measurements vary with warmup and host load. The final GPU speedup is 3.52×;
an earlier capture was 4.43×. Neither number represents complete-game FPS.
The semi-active test measures conservation of its own changed Hamiltonian,
not fidelity to the full model or long-term accretion morphology.
